This month in our towns

Are home sales up or down around here?

922 homes sold across our towns from August 1 through October 28. Last year at the same time, 976 sold.

The typical price rose about 3%, to $344,350. And most homes still sold in about a month.

One home, one story

The house with the wrong kind of deed

A seller in Travelers Rest called me about selling his home. I'll call him Joe. That's not his real name.

Joe had bought the house at a tax sale. That's when the county sells a property because the taxes weren't paid. It can be a good deal. But the deed you get from a tax sale isn't the kind most buyers can close on.

Before Joe could sell, an attorney had to make sure nobody else had any claim to the property. That's a legal process of its own, and it takes time.

We talked about this before we ever listed. So we got the attorney started right away.

The title work took longer than anyone expected. Our first buyer couldn't wait. The closing date moved again and again, and in the end that contract ended. We kept going.

Our second buyer had a deadline of his own. He was doing a 1031 exchange. That's a tax rule for people selling an investment property: they must buy the next one by a set date. By then, the title work was almost finished. The buyer agreed to close on time, and the attorney finished the work shortly after. Today the new owner has the right kind of deed.

That same month, I helped Joe buy his next home.

Joe and I still work together. At the end of 2025, I helped him buy a few more tax sale properties. Some are homes, and some are lots. A few will be going on the market soon.

The lesson for sellers: if anything about how you got your home is unusual, talk to an agent before you list. Problems with the deed can be fixed, but they take time. It's much easier to start early than to explain a delay to a buyer who's waiting.

Seller tip

Fix it, or leave it?

Sellers ask me this all the time. My answer has three parts.

Fix what's broken. Roof leaks, plumbing problems, anything structural, anything clearly wrong. A buyer's inspection will find these, and you'll likely have to fix them before closing anyway. Fixing them first means fewer surprises, and buyers feel better about the home.

Clean everything. A deep clean, carpet cleaning where it's needed, and clearing clutter. It costs little, and the home looks its best in photos and showings.

Hold off on updates that are a matter of taste. Paint colors, new carpet, new flooring. You might paint the rooms when your buyer would rather have new carpet. You might pick yellow when your buyer loves blue. Wait and see what your buyer actually wants.

If a buyer asks for one of those updates, we can handle it in the deal. Usually that's a credit toward the buyer's closing costs, or a lower price, so the buyer can make the change their own way. If the buyer has a mortgage, the lender sets limits on credits, and I'll walk you through them.

That way, you spend money only where it matters.
